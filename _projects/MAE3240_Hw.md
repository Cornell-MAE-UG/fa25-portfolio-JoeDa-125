From this homework, I practiced how to effectively utilize **thermal resistance networks**, along with how to analyze elementary fins and their uses for cooling. In the photovoltaic panel problem, conduction through different materials, contact resistance, and surface heat losses through convection and radiation are all combined into one thermal resistance network. The problem demonstrated how complicated heat transfer processes can be simplified by the thermal resistance model that allows temperature and heat fluxes to be calculated systematically. The problem also introduced the concept that solar performance can be affected by thermal conditions, as the efficiency depended on the temperature of the silicon.

This problem set also helped strengthen my foundations in **extended surface (fin) heat transfer** by looking at a system consisting of a rod and heat source with insulation at the base and end. This problem emphasized deriving temperature expressions based on conduction along a solid combined with convective heat loss to the environment. The fin element of this problem reinforced the idea of fin efficiency, fin effectiveness, and thermal resistance.
